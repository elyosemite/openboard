## Context

ADR 0004 kept the Administrator out of every Organization's data unless given an Organization Membership. That made the Instance-level role awkward: an Administrator creating, restoring or rescuing an Organization could not see what they were managing, and creating an Organization would have needed a separate person to become its Organization Owner.

It also promised more than a self-hosted product can keep: whoever administers the installation can read the database directly, so withholding data in the application protects nothing from them. Azure DevOps gives its organization administrators direct access to every project; Jira lets its administrators grant themselves access to any project. Neither treats the top administrator as unable to reach the data.

## Decision

1. An **Administrator** acts as **Organization Owner** in every **Organization**, without an **Organization Membership**, in the same way an Organization Owner acts as Workspace Owner in every Workspace of their Organization.
2. Administrators **never count** toward an Organization's Organization Owners: every Organization still needs at least one Organization Owner through an Organization Membership, so it keeps working when the Administrator changes.
3. Authorization checks treat the Administrator as an Organization Owner of the Organization being accessed. This replaces the "no Administrator bypass" instruction of ADR 0004.
4. Organizations stay isolated from each other (ADR 0003): this decision is about the Instance-level role, not about data crossing Organizations.

## Consequences

- Creating, restoring and rescuing Organizations work without extra Memberships.
- Granting the Administrator role is granting access to all data on the Instance; it must go only to people trusted with it.
- In a Cloud Instance, the operator's Administrators can read every customer's work. Trust in a Cloud offering then rests on who holds that role and on operational safeguards, such as an audit log of Administrator access, which needs its own decision before a Cloud offering exists.
